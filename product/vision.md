# Empire Wheel Vision

## Product Thesis

Empire Wheel is an interactive learning platform for understanding how civilizations acquire, organize, exercise, and transfer power.

World history is organized as a wheel of hegemonic civilizations. Each spoke represents a civilization or hegemonic system; the transitions between spokes represent the historical handoff of power.

Learners move through the wheel chronologically, investigating not only what happened, but why one system became dominant, how it functioned, what evidence survives, and why power eventually moved elsewhere.

## The Experience

Empire Wheel is not a textbook divided into webpages.

Each civilization is a substantial guided curriculum composed of approximately ten modules. Each module should provide roughly 2–5 hours of meaningful engagement through a combination of:

* original historical narrative
* academic scholarship
* archaeological and primary evidence
* maps and spatial exploration
* images and reconstructions
* embedded lectures and video
* timelines
* economic and institutional visualizations
* historical argument exercises
* AI-assisted critique
* study guides and review

A learner should repeatedly move between narrative, evidence, visualization, and interpretation.

## Core Learning Loop

Every module follows the same broad progression:

**Orient → Learn → Read → See → Explore → Examine → Argue → Review → Study**

The exact content may vary by historical period, but the intellectual process remains recognizable.

### Orient

Establish the period, geography, major actors, and central historical question.

### Learn

Provide a coherent narrative explaining the events and systems necessary to understand the module.

### Read

Direct learners toward selected scholarship and accessible academic material.

### See

Use artifacts, archaeological sites, reconstructions, diagrams, photographs, and other visual evidence to make the civilization tangible.

### Explore

Allow learners to investigate geography, settlements, trade networks, military campaigns, territorial expansion, and other spatial relationships.

### Examine

Present historical evidence and ask what conclusions the evidence does and does not support.

Evidence may include texts, inscriptions, administrative records, archaeological sites, artifacts, iconography, maps, or quantitative information.

### Argue

Require the learner to make a historical claim and support it with evidence.

### Review

Provide structured feedback evaluating historical accuracy, use of evidence, reasoning, uncertainty, and unsupported inference.

### Study

Conclude with a persistent study guide containing the chronology, concepts, people, places, evidence, debates, and questions the learner should now understand.

## The Empire Wheel

The top-level interface represents the historical curriculum as a wheel.

Each spoke represents a civilization or hegemonic period.

Selecting a spoke opens its curriculum.

Progress through that curriculum is reflected directly on the wheel.

Transitions between civilizations are first-class historical subjects rather than empty chronological boundaries. Empire Wheel should continually ask:

**Why did the baton move?**

The long-term experience should allow learners to compare civilizations across recurring dimensions such as:

* geography
* population and production
* surplus and wealth
* political institutions
* religion and legitimacy
* military organization
* trade and transportation
* information and administration
* technology
* causes of hegemonic ascent and decline

## Visual History

Empire Wheel should make historical systems visible.

Important recurring visual experiences include:

### Places

Maps, city plans, archaeological sites, reconstructions, and important landscapes.

### Movement

Trade routes, migrations, military campaigns, territorial expansion, and communication networks.

### Production

Interactive representations of how food, labor, commodities, taxation, and wealth moved through a society.

### Power

Visual models showing which institutions controlled resources and how economic capacity became political, religious, or military power.

These visual systems should be reusable across civilizations so learners can compare structures separated by thousands of years.

## Scholarship and Sources

Empire Wheel should connect learners directly with serious historical scholarship rather than hide scholarship behind simplified summaries.

Where practical, modules should link to accessible academic papers, books, museum collections, archives, university resources, translations, lectures, and other high-quality external material.

Empire Wheel must distinguish between:

1. what surviving evidence directly shows,
2. how scholars interpret that evidence,
3. competing scholarly interpretations, and
4. Empire Wheel's own synthesis or argument.

The application must never invent quotations, citations, artifacts, translations, or historical evidence.

## Learning Outcome

Completing a module should mean more than reaching the bottom of a webpage.

A learner should leave able to:

* explain the central historical sequence,
* locate the civilization geographically and chronologically,
* recognize important evidence,
* explain competing interpretations,
* connect institutions to economic and military power,
* make a defensible historical argument,
* identify uncertainty in that argument, and
* explain how the module contributes to the larger hegemonic transition represented by the Empire Wheel.

## Initial Product

The first complete curriculum is Ancient Mesopotamia.

It will establish the content model, learning loop, visual grammar, evidence system, progress system, and study experience that subsequent civilization spokes reuse.

Version 0.1 should prove one thing:

**A learner can enter a historical period knowing relatively little and emerge several hours later capable of making and defending a substantially more sophisticated historical argument.**

## Product Principle

**Don't just consume history. Interrogate it.**

Narrative provides the story.

Evidence constrains the story.

Comparison reveals the system.

Argument demonstrates understanding.
