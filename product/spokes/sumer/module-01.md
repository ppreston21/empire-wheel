# Sumer · Module 1 brief

## Before Sumer: Ubaid to Uruk

**Status:** curriculum draft; not historically reviewed  
**Scope:** southern Mesopotamia, approximately the Ubaid through Late Uruk
periods  
**Target time:** 3 hours 30 minutes  
**Spoke role:** inheritance — establishes the social and material conditions
from which the first urban system emerged

## Central question

**What changed when southern Mesopotamian communities began organizing people,
materials, and information at urban scale?**

This wording replaces a technologically deterministic question about irrigation
“requiring” government. The module should test relationships among ecology,
settlement, institutions, labor, exchange, and administrative practices without
assuming that any one factor caused cities.

## Learner outcomes

By the end, a learner can:

1. locate southern Mesopotamia and describe the module's chronological limits;
2. compare Ubaid settlement and institutional patterns with evidence associated
   with Uruk's larger scale;
3. use at least three named forms of archaeological evidence to support a claim;
4. distinguish an observation from an inference about coordination or control;
5. explain why “urban revolution” is an interpretation to evaluate rather than
   a neutral description of a single event.

## Narrative architecture

The published narrative should be short enough that encounters with evidence do
the intellectual work. It needs four sourced sections:

1. **A changing alluvial landscape** — introduce environmental possibilities
   and constraints without claiming a fixed ancient shoreline or watercourse.
2. **Ubaid inheritances** — settlements, shared material practices, buildings,
   and regional connections; avoid treating the period as merely “pre-Uruk.”
3. **Uruk at another scale** — settlement growth, differentiated activity,
   monumental building, exchange, and administrative media.
4. **What remains uncertain** — connect material patterns to competing accounts
   of authority, institutions, households, and labor.

## Evidence lab acquisition brief

Research must select exact, stable records rather than treating these categories
as evidence by themselves:

| Slot | Needed object | Learner question | Publication gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A · Place | Reviewed excavation or survey record for an Ubaid/Uruk site | What spatial change can actually be observed? | Site, phase, excavator/publication, plan rights checked |
| B · Administration | Catalogued sealing, token, numerical tablet, or proto-cuneiform tablet | What action does this record, and what does it not tell us about power? | Collection/catalogue locator and description checked |
| C · Production | Catalogued mass-produced or standardized object assemblage | Does standardization demonstrate centralized control? | Provenance, date, image rights, and scholarly interpretation checked |
| D · Synthesis | Peer-reviewed settlement or landscape dataset/map | How does scale change the argument? | Methods, date ranges, uncertainty, and reuse terms checked |

## Reading and media acquisition criteria

- Prefer an open scholarly synthesis plus a contrasting specialist argument.
- Prefer museum, excavation, university, or research-database object pages for
  evidence; use general explainers only for orientation.
- Select excerpts by learner purpose, not fame. Record the relevant chapter or
  page range and expected reading time.
- Check accessibility without institutional login and provide a fallback.
- Record full citation metadata, stable URL/identifier, rights, and check date.
- Do not embed video until creator, duration, availability, and pedagogical
  purpose are verified.

## Argument and rubric

**Prompt:** Which combination of evidence best explains the changing scale of
coordination between the Ubaid and Uruk periods? Make a causal claim, use three
named evidence objects, distinguish observation from inference, and explain one
important limitation or competing interpretation.

Review scores historical accuracy, evidence use, causal reasoning, explicit
inference, and treatment of uncertainty. Completion requires the evidence table
and an argument; opening every resource is not mastery.

## Historical QA questions

- Are chronology and geographic labels consistent with the cited scholarship?
- Does the module avoid equating temples, monumental buildings, and centralized
  government without evidence?
- Does it avoid presenting irrigation as a single-cause explanation?
- Are “city,” “state,” “specialist,” and “temple economy” defined and debated?
- Can every displayed claim, object, map layer, and image be traced to its source?
- Are absent voices and the limits of archaeological inference made explicit?
